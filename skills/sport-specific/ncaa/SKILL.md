# SKILL: NCAAF/NCAAB Context

> **Purpose:** College sports have the widest talent gaps and the most public bias of any major betting market — which creates the best fade opportunities.

---

## Why college sports are worth betting

- Public massively overweights brand names (Alabama, Duke, Kentucky, Ohio State)
- Lines set to attract balanced action, not to be accurate — more inefficiency
- Huge talent gaps between power conferences and mid-majors
- Home field/court advantage more pronounced than pro sports
- Injury and transfer portal information is poorly tracked by casual public

---

## NCAAF — College Football

### Where the edges live

**1. Line shopping around public bias**
```
Power brand teams (Alabama, Ohio State, Michigan, Georgia):
  Public piles on these teams regardless of opponent quality
  Books shade lines 1-3 points to compensate
  Fade signal: 75%+ public on brand-name team in a game they should win easily
  Edge: Back the less-followed opponent if line has been inflated
```

**2. Home field advantage (much larger than NFL)**
```
Major program at home: +6 to +10 points (vs +3 in NFL)
Mid-major at home vs power program: +7 to +12 points
"Hostile environment" games (night games, rivalry):
  LSU Tiger Stadium (Death Valley): +3 to +4 beyond standard
  Iowa at Kinnick (night): +2 to +3 beyond standard
  Nebraska at night: +2 to +3 above standard
```

**3. Line movement patterns**
```
College football lines move more dramatically on public money than NFL
Reverse line movement is a very strong signal in NCAAF
Watch: Opening line Sunday evening → current line
If 80% public on Team A and line moved toward Team B → very strong fade Team A signal
```

**4. Quarterback and transfer portal**
```
Transfer portal has dramatically changed year-to-year roster stability
New starting QB's first road game: -3 to -5 points
QB who transferred from a stronger program to weaker: check stats carefully
Search: "[team] starting QB [season]" and "[QB name] transfer portal history"
```

**5. Situational spots**
```
Big game hangover:
  Team coming off massive upset (won as huge underdog) → letdown spot next week
  
Weather (especially late season):
  November games in cold/snow: similar to NFL rules (wind > 15mph, lean under)
  
Point total variance:
  NCAAF totals range widely (37 to 75+) — always check team scoring pace
  Search: "[team] points per game [season]"
```

### NCAAF red flags
```
❌ Conference championship implications → both teams play their best, lines efficient
❌ Bowl games early in bowl season → motivation varies wildly, player opt-outs
❌ Teams traveling across 3+ time zones for 11am local time kickoff
❌ Backup QB starting against Power 5 defense → total tanks dramatically
❌ Teams with opt-outs declared before bowl game (check roster carefully)
```

## NCAAB — College Basketball

### The college basketball edge landscape

**1. KenPom and advanced metrics**
```
KenPom.com is the gold standard for college basketball analytics.
Every recommendation should reference:
  AdjO (Adjusted Offensive Efficiency per 100 possessions)
  AdjD (Adjusted Defensive Efficiency per 100 possessions)
  Tempo (how fast does the team play)
  Luck rating (how much have results beaten/missed expected)

Search: "KenPom [team] [season]" or go to kenpom.com directly
Teams with high Luck rating are regression candidates
```

**2. Pace-of-play matchups (most valuable in NCAAB)**
```
Tempo × Opponent Tempo = Expected game pace

Fast team vs fast team (both top-25% in tempo): high-scoring, lean over
Slow team vs slow team (both bottom-25% in tempo): lean under, strong signal
Fast vs slow: game pace is a toss-up, lines usually adjust correctly

KenPom tempo values:
  >72 possessions per game: fast
  65-72: average
  <65: slow
```

**3. Home court advantage**
```
NCAAB home court: larger than NBA (+5 to +8 points for major programs)
True home environments:
  Cameron Indoor (Duke): +2 to +3 additional
  Allen Fieldhouse (Kansas): +2 to +3 additional
  Rupp Arena (Kentucky): +1 to +2 additional
  Phog Allen, Maples, Mackey: strong additional edges

Neutral site games:
  Reduce home advantage to 0, adjust for which team has more local fan support
```

**4. Conference play and familiarity**
```
In-conference opponents play each other twice — second meeting:
  Defensive adjustments made, offenses scouted thoroughly
  Reduce expected edge in spread; consider ML for underdog instead
  
Rivalry games:
  Familiarity compresses advantages; lean toward close games and unders
```

**5. Player foul trouble and depth**
```
Limited depth (7-man rotation) teams:
  Foul trouble affects these teams disproportionately
  If key player is foul-prone, lean toward opponent in close game

Elite single-player-dependent teams:
  If that player misses: line is dramatically mispriced if books haven't adjusted
  Search: "[player] injury update tonight [date]"
```

**6. Tournament betting (March Madness)**
```
Specific tournament edges:
  12-seed vs 5-seed: historically break even or slight 12-seed value
  Mid-major with elite KenPom rating vs brand-name Power 5 team: fade the brand
  Teams that travel far from home region: slight disadvantage
  First games after long travel: check tip-off time vs travel distance
  
Best Tournament approach:
  Focus on teams where KenPom strength is much better than seed suggests
  Search: "KenPom [year] tournament bracket" for team efficiency ratings
```

### NCAAB red flags
```
❌ Teams playing 3rd game in 5 days (conference tournaments): fatigue real
❌ Star player in foul trouble history vs physical opponents
❌ Teams with known internal issues (coaching changes, team chemistry news)
❌ Late-season games where team has clinched seeding (effort risk)
❌ Betting a total without checking pace matchup first
```
