# Glossary

Key terms used throughout the skill files, defined plainly.

---

**ATS (Against the Spread)**
Whether a team won or lost after the point spread is applied. A team can win the game but lose ATS if they win by less than the spread. ATS records reveal how well a team performs relative to expectations.

**Bankroll**
The total money you have set aside specifically for betting. Never bet money you can't afford to lose. Your bankroll is the foundation — every other number in the system (unit size, Kelly stake, stop-loss) is a percentage of it.

**Brier Score**
A measure of probability forecast accuracy. Lower is better. Ranges from 0 (perfect) to 1 (maximally wrong). Used in the performance tracking skill to tell you if your probability estimates are calibrated.

**CLV (Closing Line Value)**
The difference between the line you bet and where the line closed before game time. Positive CLV (you got a better number than the market settled at) is the best long-term indicator that you're finding real edge. Professional bettors track this obsessively.

**Corsi / CF%**
A hockey possession metric: the percentage of shot attempts (for and against) that belong to your team at even strength. CF% above 55% indicates puck possession dominance. Used in the NHL skill file.

**Vig (also Juice or Overround)**
The sportsbook's commission built into every line. At -110/-110, both sides imply 52.38% probability — totaling 104.76%. The extra 4.76% is the book's take. You must remove vig before calculating true market probabilities.

**DVOA (Defense-adjusted Value Over Average)**
A Football Outsiders metric measuring NFL team efficiency on a per-play basis, adjusted for opponents. More predictive than basic stats like yards or points. Used in the NFL skill file.

**EV (Expected Value)**
The average outcome per bet if you made the same bet infinite times. Positive EV (+EV) means you expect to profit over a large sample. Every bet in this system requires EV above the threshold to qualify.

**Edge**
The gap between your estimated probability of winning and the market's implied probability (after vig removal). Edge = Your P(win) − Market P(win). Positive edge means the market is underpricing your side.

**FIP (Fielding Independent Pitching)**
A baseball metric that strips out defense and luck, measuring only outcomes pitchers directly control: strikeouts, walks, and home runs. More predictive than ERA. Used in the MLB skill file.

**Kelly Criterion**
A mathematical formula for optimal bet sizing. Maximizes long-term bankroll growth when your edge and odds are correctly estimated. This system uses fractional Kelly (25% of full Kelly) to control variance.

**Key Numbers**
In football, specific margins of victory that occur most frequently due to scoring structure. 3 (field goal), 7 (touchdown), 10, and 14 are the most important. Getting or giving a half-point through a key number has outsized value.

**Line Movement**
When a sportsbook adjusts their posted odds after the initial line is set. Movement reflects incoming bets and information. Reverse line movement — line moving against the direction of public betting percentage — is a sharp money signal.

**Load Management**
When NBA teams rest healthy star players to manage their workload over an 82-game season. Common with older stars or those recovering from injury. Critical to track in the NBA skill file.

**ML (Moneyline)**
Betting on which team wins outright, with no point spread. Expressed in American odds (+150 means $100 bet wins $150 profit; -150 means you must bet $150 to win $100 profit).

**Opener**
The first line a sportsbook posts, typically days before game time. Comparing opener to current line reveals what information and which bettors have moved it.

**PDO**
A hockey metric: team shooting percentage plus goalie save percentage. League average is always 100%. Teams above 104 are lucky and due for regression; below 96 are unlucky and likely to improve. Used in the NHL skill file.

**Point Spread**
The margin of victory a team must win by (if favored) or can lose by (if an underdog) for a bet to cash. Bills -3.5 means Buffalo must win by 4 or more for a bet on them to win.

**Public Money / Public Action**
Bets from casual bettors, typically flowing toward popular teams, favorites, and primetime games. Books shade lines to account for public bias. Fading public consensus (when backed by a model edge) is a legitimate strategy.

**Reverse Line Movement (RLM)**
When the line moves opposite to the public betting percentage. If 70% of bets are on the favorite, but the line moves toward the underdog, sharp money is on the underdog. Strong signal.

**Sharp / Sharp Money**
Wagers placed by professional or sophisticated bettors who win long-term. Books adjust lines in response to sharp action regardless of the dollar volume of public action. Finding where sharps are betting is a strong directional signal.

**Total (Over/Under)**
Betting on the combined score of both teams. The book sets a number (e.g., 47.5 in NFL); you bet whether the actual combined score will be over or under that number.

**Unit**
The standard measurement for bet sizing. In this system, 1 unit = 2% of bankroll. All bet recommendations are expressed in units so the system works at any bankroll size.

**Vig-Removed Probability**
The market's true implied win probability after stripping out the sportsbook's margin. If both sides are -110, the vig-removed probability for each side is 50%, not 52.38%.

**xFIP (Expected FIP)**
Like FIP, but also normalizes home run rate to league average, since home runs on balls in play have a luck component. The most stable single-season ERA estimator available. Used in the MLB skill file.
